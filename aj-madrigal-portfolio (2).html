Compliance

Rejections are usually structural, not chemical.

A compliant product still gets pulled when the catalogue around it is wrong — a variant that drags the displayed starting price, an image with text overlay, a missing SDS on a hazmat line. I work the documentation and the structure together, because fixing one without the other just moves the violation.

  • Safety Data Sheet and hazmat submissions, including multi-document PDF merges
  • Food labeling requirements and product image compliance
  • Listing rejection diagnosis where the cause is variant, pricing or catalogue structure
  • Return policy content, with enforceability limits flagged before publication
Shop health

One unactioned refund can cost you a whole point.

Most score drops look systemic and aren't. The work is finding which pillar moved and why before touching anything else — then executing the recovery in order, so the fix is attributable rather than hopeful.

  • SPS monitoring and pillar-level diagnosis across the portfolio
  • Negative review replies, ratings management and review cluster analysis
  • Refund and cancellation follow-up on the customer service pillar
  • Chatbot FAQ configuration to cut response-time exposure
Integration

Native connector or Ownbase — decided per brand.

Sync failures rarely announce themselves. They show up as an order that never reached the 3PL or tracking that never posted back, days after the fact. I establish which path a brand runs on, then trace the break to the mapping rather than guessing at the platform.

  • Product mapping errors and legacy listings pointed at the wrong Shopify product
  • Failed orders and tracking that does not post back to TikTok
  • Fulfillment handoff breakdowns between Shopify, 3PL and the marketplace
  • Migration onto Ownbase where the native connector keeps failing

FAQ

Questions, answered.

A shop score dropped overnight. Where do you start?

With the pillar, not the products. Most drops trace to customer service — slow refunds or unanswered negative reviews — rather than anything systemic. On CTRL a fall from 3.6 to 2.8 came down to a single unactioned weekend refund on one SKU. Finding that first means the recovery plan is ordered and attributable instead of scattershot.

My product is compliant but the listing keeps getting rejected. Why?

Usually the catalogue around it. On Completely Bare, Bikini Bump Blaster was rejected for a misleading starting price caused by the Travel Size variant — the product was fine, the variant structure wasn't. Removing that variant from the TikTok channel listing only, leaving the Shopify product intact, resolved it without touching the storefront.

Native TikTok connector or Ownbase?

It depends on the brand, and the first job is establishing which one is actually running. Where the native connector keeps producing failed orders or tracking that never posts back, migration is the answer — that's what resolved persistent sync failures for Vai Dai Coffee. Where it's stable, the break is usually a mapping error, like a restored legacy listing pointed at the wrong Shopify product.

How do you keep twelve brands auditable at once?

Three layers. A daily audit across every brand logging to the Master Tracker, run through a suite of prompt shortcuts rather than by hand. A weekly pass through nine Seller Center pages per brand against a fifteen-tab workbook. Then reporting on top of both. The point of the infrastructure is that nothing depends on remembering.

What happens when a brand leaves the portfolio?

A structured offboarding rather than a handover email — full app inventory audit and an exit document, so whoever picks it up inherits the state of the account rather than a guess at it. That's what was compiled for Fatboy Hair ahead of the August 2026 exit.
