Compliance

Rejections are usually structural, not chemical.

A compliant product still gets pulled when the catalogue around it is wrong — a variant that drags the displayed starting price, an image with text overlay, a missing SDS on a hazmat line. I work the documentation and the structure together, because fixing one without the other just moves the violation.

  • Safety Data Sheet and hazmat submissions, including multi-document PDF merges
  • Food labeling requirements and product image compliance
  • Listing rejection diagnosis where the cause is variant, pricing or catalogue structure
  • Return policy content, with enforceability limits flagged before publication
Shop health

One unactioned refund can cost you a whole point.

Most score drops look systemic and aren't. The work is finding which pillar moved and why before touching anything else — then executing the recovery in order, so the fix is attributable rather than hopeful.

  • SPS monitoring and pillar-level diagnosis across the portfolio
  • Negative review replies, ratings management and review cluster analysis
  • Refund and cancellation follow-up on the customer service pillar
  • Chatbot FAQ configuration to cut response-time exposure
Affiliate

A sample is inventory, not a giveaway.

Most affiliate programs leak at the same two points: requests pile up unanswered until creators stop asking, and product ships to people who never post. The work is a queue that gets cleared on a cadence and an approval bar that ties sample cost to expected output, then following through on who actually delivered.

  • Open Collaboration setup, commission benchmarking and sample SLA configuration
  • Queue triage at volume, approving against estimated post rate and projected GMV
  • Outreach automation built as invite plus DM sequences, gated by creator GMV band
  • Seeding decisions made against sample inventory and cost, tracked to posting activity
  • Spark code capture from posting affiliates, then boosting what already works
Integration

Native connector or a sync app — decided per brand.

Every brand runs on one of two paths: the native TikTok connector, or a third-party sync app such as Ownbase. Sync failures rarely announce themselves either way. They show up as an order that never reached the 3PL or tracking that never posted back, days after the fact. I establish which path a brand is actually on, then trace the break to the mapping rather than guessing at the platform.

  • Product mapping errors and legacy listings pointed at the wrong Shopify product
  • Failed orders and tracking that does not post back to TikTok
  • Fulfillment handoff breakdowns between Shopify, 3PL and the marketplace
  • Migration onto a sync app where the native connector keeps failing
  • Escalation to the app vendor when the defect is in their import, not our setup
  • Install, connection and mapping setup when a brand moves onto a new sync path

Before the agency

The first shop I ran was my own.

I built and operated my own TikTok Shop in the Philippines reselling thrifted clothing, mostly jackets, back when branded and luxury pieces could still be shown on the platform. It started at zero, sold entirely through LIVE, and grew into a team.

₱500KIn sales from a standing start, built through LIVE selling rather than paid traffic
13People on the floor across packing, LIVE hosting, product sorting and customer service
2Hosts per LIVE room, running morning and evening shifts so the room was never dark
What it taught

Everything since has been the same problem at bigger scale.

Running the shop meant owning every part of it at once: sourcing, listing, hosting, packing, replying to customers, and staying inside platform rules that kept changing underneath the category. That is where the operational habits came from: the shift structure, the daily checks, and the instinct to look at the process before blaming the product.

  • LIVE selling as the primary sales channel, staffed and scheduled like a shift operation rather than run ad hoc
  • Roles split by function across a 13-person team, with morning and evening coverage on the same catalogue
  • Sourcing and grading secondhand inventory where every unit is effectively a one-off SKU
  • Order accuracy, packing and after-sales handled in-house, with customer service treated as part of the sales floor
  • Operating inside branded and luxury resale rules on TikTok, and adapting when the category tightened

Systems

The infrastructure the work runs on.

Designed, built and maintained in-house so portfolio-scale operations run on process rather than memory.

Late shipment early warning Runs daily

Scheduled agent that reads the Master Tracker every morning and reports which brands have overdue orders and which have orders sitting inside the 24-hour window, then drafts a per-brand Slack message and pushes a summary notification. It separates a fulfillment stall from a warehouse backlog by checking whether overdue orders have tracking posted back, flags brands whose rows have gone stale rather than treating an old check as current, and reconciles the sheet's Philippine-time dates against the snapshot time so counts are read correctly. Nothing posts without review.

SPS alert report Runs daily

Scheduled agent that pulls Shop Performance Score for every rated brand, flags anything below threshold, and writes a full account health report to Drive. It checks the last report actually posted in Slack rather than the last one written, so a doc sitting unsent is caught, diagnoses the pillar behind the drop down to the underlying metric, and pulls stock state so the report either carries a restock ask or explicitly does not. Judgment calls, including ambiguous brand abbreviations in the fix log, are surfaced rather than silently resolved.

TikTok Shop Master Tracker

Google Sheets tracker with a Daily Brand Tracker, Daily Order Monitor and Stock Monitor. Twelve brands prefilled with automatic flagging against stock health thresholds, and conditional formatting that surfaces any non-zero order exception in red.

Daily audit prompt suite

Claude in Chrome shortcuts covering the full daily routine, from the summary update through nine sequential audit steps plus a dedicated SPS pull. Hardened with a text-only reading rule to avoid image processing failures, and explicit sheet write rules to survive background tab throttling.

Weekly performance tracker

Fifteen-tab Google Sheets workbook paired with an audit prompt that walks nine Seller Center pages per brand, converting a full-day manual review into a structured weekly pass.

Sales Accelerator tracker In development

Standalone workbook with a brand-level rollup and a product-level detail tab recording qualification status, points earned, missing requirements, program tasks with completion state, and rewards gained.

SOP library

Brand-level standard operating procedures covering recurring processes such as brand authorization, compliance submissions and sync troubleshooting.

Workflow documentation

Submitted the TikTok Shop Late Shipment Early Warning workflow through the internal idea intake process, since built and now running daily, and established the ecommerce and TikTok Shop section of the company product mapping sheet.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

A shop score dropped overnight. Where do you start?

With the pillar, not the products. Most drops trace to customer service — slow refunds or unanswered negative reviews — rather than anything systemic. On CTRL a fall from 3.6 to 2.8 came down to a single unactioned weekend refund on one SKU. Finding that first means the recovery plan is ordered and attributable instead of scattershot.

My product is compliant but the listing keeps getting rejected. Why?

Usually the catalogue around it. On Completely Bare, Bikini Bump Blaster was rejected for a misleading starting price caused by the Travel Size variant — the product was fine, the variant structure wasn't. Removing that variant from the TikTok channel listing only, leaving the Shopify product intact, resolved it without touching the storefront.

Native connector or a third-party sync app?

It depends on the brand, and the first job is establishing which one is actually running. Where the native connector keeps producing failed orders or tracking that never posts back, migrating to an app is the answer — that's what resolved persistent sync failures for Vai Dai Coffee. Where it's stable, the break is usually a mapping error, like a restored legacy listing pointed at the wrong Shopify product.

How do you keep twenty-three brands auditable at once?

Three layers. A daily audit across every brand logging to the Master Tracker, run through a suite of prompt shortcuts rather than by hand. A weekly pass through nine Seller Center pages per brand against a fifteen-tab workbook. Then reporting on top of both. The point of the infrastructure is that nothing depends on remembering.

What do you do when the problem isn't yours to fix?

Document it well enough that the party who can fix it will. On Vai Dai Coffee, free sample orders were importing with the line item zeroed out, so the product value vanished from the record. Rather than patching it brand-side, I put the broken order next to a correct one from the same week, showed the exact structural difference, and sent both to the app vendor. They changed the import behaviour in the product. The same principle applies to platform escalations: a trademark case or a verification block moves when the ask is specific and evidenced, not when it's chased.

What have you actually automated?

The gathering, not the judgment. Two agents run on a schedule every day — one reports which brands have overdue orders and which are inside the 24-hour window, the other pulls shop performance score and writes the account health report. Both draft the Slack message and stop. What that buys is not saved typing, it's that the check happens whether or not I remember to run it, and edge cases get surfaced rather than skipped: a brand whose data has gone stale, a report written but never posted, a fix-log row filed under an abbreviation that could belong to two brands.

How do you stop an affiliate program burning through samples?

By treating the sample as inventory with a cost attached. Requests get filtered to recommended creators and approved against estimated post rate and projected GMV rather than approved on arrival, auto-approve stays off, and the invite carries a content requirement and an expiry. Then the follow-through matters more than the send: tracking who posted separates creators worth seeding again from those who took product and went quiet.

What happens when a brand leaves the portfolio?

A structured offboarding rather than a handover email — full app inventory audit and an exit document, so whoever picks it up inherits the state of the account rather than a guess at it. That's what was compiled for Fatboy Hair ahead of the August 2026 exit.
